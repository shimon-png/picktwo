About You & Taste Profile
Crowd Pick · plan · Nov 25, 2026

Ask who they are one tap at a time, and pay them back right away

Guests now get 15 free votes before the sign-up wall. Today we learn nothing about them until they sign up, so most visitors leave as anonymous devices we can't target, retarget or put in a brand panel. This plan fixes that with three things: short "about you" cards inside the deck, a "people like you" result that only works once they answer, and a taste profile they earn by voting. Passive signals (region, phone, time of day, estimated income) fill in the rest for everyone, including people who answer nothing.

1 · All on one card, or spread out?

Spread out. One question per card, earned by the vote before it.

A single form of four questions reads like a survey, and people skip surveys. A single one-tap card between battles feels like part of the game. It also lets each answer pay off on the very next result, which teaches people that answering is worth it. The rules:

  • An about-you card only appears right after a result, as the next card in the deck. Same size, same tap targets, same Arcade Night look as a battle.
  • Never two cards in a row, and at least two battles between asks.
  • Every card has Skip. A skipped question comes back once, 10 votes later, then only lives in Settings and behind the locked bar.
  • Answers from a guest are saved against their device and move onto their account when they sign up (the same way guest votes carry over today). Sign-up then skips anything already answered, so for most people it becomes email, code, username, ZIP.
  • Questions that passive data already answers well (phone type, rough region) are never asked.
  • Split and shuffle (Alex): a question with N answers splits the card into N equal slices that fill it, the same way a battle splits into two. The order is shuffled for each person and each showing, so no answer gets the top or first spot more often. The order shown is logged on about_card_shown, so the Brain can check for position bias. Age uses the same six bands sign-up and targeting already use (18–24 up to 65+), so the data lines up.

4 · What they get

A taste profile in You, and a card worth posting

The artifact is a Taste profile: a six-trait wheel built from what they picked, a name for their type, and how they compare with people like them. It fills in as they vote (2 traits at vote 10, all 6 at vote 30), which gives guests a reason to sign up ("save your profile") and members a reason to keep voting. A one-tap share card sized for Instagram and TikTok stories brings new people in.

What drives each trait

We already have the raw material. Every new battle is auto-tagged by AI with up to three voter traits per side (battle.tags.brain.a / .b, 21 traits in lib/brain-tags.ts). A pick "votes for" the traits on the side they chose and "against" the traits on the side they passed on. Score = (for − against) ÷ battles that carried that trait, smoothed toward 0 until there are 5 such battles, then shown against the crowd average. Same pure lift math style as taste-rules.ts, with tests.

The other traits we already tag (family_first, career_driven, social, independent, health_focused, eco_conscious, tech_forward) appear as badges once they're strong, and all 21 feed brand panels. The type name comes from the top two axes (6 × 6 table of names like "Romantic realist", "Careful splurger"), written by hand, not generated. Battles posted before AI tagging went live may have no tags; a one-time back-fill fixes that.

5 · Free signals

Region, phone, time of day and estimated income, for every visitor

These cost nothing and need no question, so they cover the guests who answer nothing. I checked what the app logs today. The browser already sends a session_start event (lib/events.ts), and the server stamps it with the raw user-agent in server/analytics.ts. Here is what's there and what's missing.

SignalTodayHow to get itWhere in code
Region Missing Vercel adds the visitor's location to every request as headers: x-vercel-ip-country, -country-region (state), -city, -postal-code, -timezone. Free, no lookup service. City and ZIP from IP are metro-level guesses (mobile carriers often place people a city away), so we store them as "IP guess", never overwrite a typed ZIP. app/api/events/route.ts reads the headers (or geolocation() from @vercel/functions) and passes them into logClientEvents, which adds them to session_start. Also saved on the device row.
Time of day Have it Server time plus the browser's time zone (tz, already in session_start) gives local hour and weekday for every event. No capture work. Add a local_hour helper in the Brain queries.
iPhone vs Android, OS Raw only The user-agent string is stored but never parsed. Parse it once into platform (iOS / Android / desktop), browser, and in-app browser (X, Instagram, TikTok all mark their UA, which also tells us where they came from). Pure parseDevice(ua) in lib/analytics-rules.ts (next to isBotUserAgent), with tests. Store the parsed result on session_start and the device row.
Newer vs older phone Missing iPhone: Apple hides the model, but screen size × pixel density narrows it to a class: 430×932 @3x = Pro Max / Plus (newest, priciest), 393×852 @3x = recent Pro / standard, 375×667 @2x = SE or 8 (budget or old). We log the viewport today, not the screen, so add screen.width, screen.height, devicePixelRatio. Android (Chrome): ask for the model with client hints, e.g. "SM-S928U" = Galaxy S24 Ultra; plus navigator.deviceMemory. A small lookup table maps model class to a price tier (budget / mid / flagship). startProps() in lib/events.ts adds screen, dpr, memory and navigator.userAgentData.getHighEntropyValues(["model","platformVersion"]). Tier mapping in a pure deviceTier(), tested.
Estimated income Missing Map ZIP (typed, or IP guess) to the Census American Community Survey 5-year median household income for that ZIP area (table B19013), plus % renters (B25003). Public, free, about 33,000 rows. Combine with phone tier into an income band (lower / middle / upper) used only for weighting and brand panels, never shown to anyone. Static data file shipped server-side like the zipcodes dataset in services/zip.ts. New pure incomeBand(zipIncome, deviceTier) in services/demo-rules.ts.
Ad pixels (retarget guests) X only X's pixel is live with the sign-up conversion. Adding Meta's pixel (and later its server-side Conversions API) lets us retarget Instagram traffic. Fire a "voted 5" event too, so ads can optimize for engaged guests, not just sign-ups. Same pattern as XPixel and lib/marketing.ts.

6 · Data and privacy

Where answers live

  • New table profile_answer: who (account or device), question, answer, when, and where it was asked (card, sign-up, Settings). Append-only, like the Brain events.
  • Current values copy onto user when they sign up (sex and age already have columns; new ones: relationship, housing, work).
  • device table gets IP region, platform and phone tier from the first visit, so guests are in the Brain and Journey views with demographics.
  • New Brain events: about_card_shown, about_card_answered, about_card_skipped, like_you_shown, taste_shared. Added to data-dictionary.md first.

Privacy cautions (short version)

  • Race, ethnicity, religion, health and sex life are "sensitive" data under California's CPRA and most new state laws. Political views are sensitive in some states. Ask only with separate, explicit opt-in, use only in groups, and let people delete them. Never as a deck card.
  • Pixels count as "sharing" under California law. Once Meta joins X, we need a "Do not sell or share my info" link and should honor the browser's Global Privacy Control signal.
  • Brand insights stay in groups: minimum 20 people per cell (the taste-profile rule already uses 20), from people with data_consent on. Never an individual, never a ZIP-level cell.
  • Privacy policy gets a plain list of what we infer (region from IP, phone type, estimated income band) and why.
